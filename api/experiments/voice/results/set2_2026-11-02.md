# Set 2: a harder, fair test of push-to-talk, always-on and Spark-on-audio (2026-11-02)

Question (CK): on harder speech, at a distance, with music in the room, which way of turning speech into marks
**ships the fewest wrong marks to the admin**, and how does the production chain compare?

Script: `api/experiments/voice/set2.py` (every stage, from clip cutting to the tables). Raw replies are in
`out/set2/` (ignored), scores in `results/set2_r1.json` and `set2_r2.json`, generated tables in
`results/set2_tables.md` (also appended below), and every paid call in `spend.jsonl` (kinds `s2:*`).

## The plain read

The numbers favour the production chain on the new, realistic recordings, but only modestly, and they do not
favour it on the augmented old 40. On set 2 (CK's 27 messy lines, iPad 2–3 m away, quiet and with Dvořák playing),
push-to-talk production (P1) got 97/108 lines right (90%) and shipped 11 wrong marks (10%). Spark on audio got
90/108 (1.2) and 89/108 (1.3), shipping 17 and 16 (15–16%). The whole gap is the music take: 49/54 for P1 against
42/54 for both audio models, which made hearing errors the ASR did not (1.2 put "Trumpets and cellos" on the
Sopranos or Basses and heard "60" as "16"; 1.3 heard 170 as 178, 88 as 8 and "Concertmaster" as Cello; both moved
"Firsts, mute on" to everyone). On the old 40 with synthetic reverb, music bed and pink noise, the chains tie on
accuracy (P1 79%, P3a 81%, P3b 80% of 240). P1 ships the **most** errors there (50 against 45 and 35), because
Voice Transcribe turns sounds into names ("Right, Inès, fermenta" on v27, "No louder, Inès 88" on v10) and hears
"Fiddles" as "Firsts" (v18) under music and noise,
while 1.3-on-audio fails safe by dropping 12 instructions. Always-on (P2) matches push-to-talk on accuracy (88–91%)
and is about 0.7 s faster at the median, but the endpointer cost lines the clips never lose. It swallowed "Violas"
at the start of h18 in both quiet runs, so the turn read "crescendo at thirty" for everyone, which the turn-alone
reading shipped once as an ensemble cresc. It also merged lines across 2–3 s pauses after an unfinished sentence
("Sopranos at bar sixty you yes you bar eighty eight too loud" became "Too loud 60–88" for the Sopranos). The
production check (verbatim quote, bars in range, known audience) refused **nothing** in any P1 or P2 reading (428 P1 lines, clean included, and every P2 turn), so it
caught none of the shipped errors. Every error that reached the tray was a plausible mark with the wrong part or
bars, which no check of this kind can see. Net: keep production for push-to-talk. The margin over Spark-on-audio
is about 6 shipped errors per 100 lines on real far-field takes and a wash on synthetic noise, and the speed case
for audio (1.8 s against 3.7 s median) stands. Most dangerous errors in every pipeline are audience errors on
addressee words (names, "Fiddles", "except"), so that is where work pays, not the choice of pipeline.

## Setup

- **Set 2 clips:** `fixtures/voice/audio2/h-quiet.wav` (238 s) and `h-music.wav` (219 s), cut into 27 clips each
  (`audio2/clips/hNN.wav`, `hNNm.wav`, mapping in `clips.json` and below). Both files are cut with the same energy
  VAD on 20 ms frames:
  - Quiet file: −45/−55 dBFS hysteresis and 1.5 s of silence give 29 segments. Segment 0 ("Yeah.", before the first
    line) is dropped. Segment 19 (the word "diminuendo" said again 1.8 s after h18) stays in h18's clip; that is
    the repeated line.
  - Music file: the gate is raised to −30/−36 dBFS with 0.7 s of silence, and segments less than 1.7 s apart are
    joined. That gives exactly 27.
  - Alignment check: every segment was transcribed once by batch Voice Transcribe (kind `s2:align:seg`, not
    scored). Each clip's transcript is closer to its own script2 line than to any other, which the script asserts.
    CK said no line numbers; nothing needed stripping. Clips are padded 0.3 s each side, at most to the middle of
    the gap.
- **Old 40 augmented** (`fixtures/voice/audio/aug/{far,musicbed,noise}/`, `augment.json`). ffmpeg and sox are not
  installed on this machine, so the augmentation is numpy, seeded per clip (seed 20261102 + index):
  - **far:** a synthetic room (RT60 0.6 s, reverberant energy equal to the direct path), then −12 dB.
  - **musicbed:** the music-only stretches of `h-music.wav` (57 s, 0.4 s in from each spoken span), at −20 dB
    relative to the speech RMS.
  - **noise:** pink noise at −25 dB relative to the speech RMS.
  - Clips whose peak passed 32000 were scaled down; CK's dry takes are already near full scale.
- **Pipelines**, with every Spark call the product's call: chat completions, `reasoning_effort: "minimal"`,
  streamed, 8192 output tokens, v4f prompt from `voice_prompt.build_prompt`, and the facts the app passes for the
  quartet + choir book (groups, landmarks `[]`).
  - **P1 push-to-talk:** the clip goes to `meta_transcribe` (DIARIZATION, the app's `Score.keywords()` with
    glossary). The transcript goes to Spark 1.3, and the answer to `checked_marks`, imported from the app (it needs
    no DB). Latency is ASR plus Spark wall time; upload and insert are not counted.
  - **P2 always-on:** the whole file is streamed at real-time pace in 80 ms PCM_16KHZ frames to
    `wss://api.meta.ai/v1/asr/realtime` (ENDPOINTING, the same keywords, no lead-in, 3 s of silence after). Spark
    is called the moment each `speechComplete` arrives. Two readings:
    - **turn alone:** each turn is read on its own.
    - **rolling group:** 013-always-on's design. A turn starting within 1 s of the last one's end joins its group
      (at most 3 turns), the group is re-read, and the group's last reading stands.
    - A mark goes to the line its quote came from. Latency runs from the line's VAD end of speech to that line's
      last reading finishing, on the stream clock.
  - **P3a / P3b:** the push-to-talk clip goes as `input_audio` to muse-spark-1.2 / 1.3, with audio_vs_asr's request
    and audio prompt. There is no transcript, so there is no check and every parsed mark ships. The tables' "alt"
    column shows what every check except the quote check would have stopped: nothing.
- **Conditions:** set 2 quiet and music for P1, P2, P3; old 40 far, musicbed and noise for P1 and P3. "clean" is
  last night's audio_vs_asr calls on the unaltered 40 (its C, A and B), rescored at no cost with this scoring. Two
  runs per cell, shuffled and interleaved, 6 calls at a time. P2 r2 streamed while batch r1 ran.
- **Scoring:**
  - **Set 2:** the messy set's matcher (`voice_text.run.match` with the messy ANY fields, every listed alternative).
    v4f has no confidence field, and production writes every surviving mark as `proposed`, so every mark reaches the
    admin and none reaches the stand. A "flag" line is right when its mark has the right core fields, or when there
    is no mark and `also` is "none". A "none" line is right with no mark, or with any mark when `also` is "flag".
  - **Old 40:** `voice_eval.score_line` (all 5 fields), with **v04c relabelled Soprano**. "w/o clipped" leaves out
    v11, v16 and v28.
  - **Shipped error:** a wrong mark reached the admin. A line that is wrong only because a mark is missing counts
    as missed.
  - **Dangerous:** a shipped mark with the wrong part or audience, the wrong bars, or the opposite instruction.
    `INVERTED` covers don't-rush, no-breath, sarcastic forte, not-a-rit., the cresc.→dim. correction, "not louder",
    "no vibrato" and "except the cello". An inverted card counts as wrong whatever its fields say.
  - **Phantom:** a mark where nothing should be written.
- **Price:** list rates, no cache discount: Spark $1.25 in and $4.25 out per M tokens; audio is 12.5 tokens per
  second inside `prompt_tokens`; Voice Transcribe $0.18 per hour.
- **Errors:**
  - Batch: no errors, and every `finish_reason` was `stop`.
  - P2 r1 first attempt: the Mac lost DNS for about a minute at 02:18. 23 Spark calls in both streams failed with
    `ConnectError: nodename nor servname provided`, and the 3-in-a-row rule stopped P2. The failure was local, not
    the pipeline. Once `api.meta.ai` resolved again, both streams were re-run from scratch. The failed records are
    kept as `out/set2/r1/P2/*.netfail.json`, and their spend (about $0.18) is counted below.

## P2: boundaries and latency

- **Endpointer lag:** `speechComplete` arrives 0.33–0.54 s after the end of speech at the median.
- **Unfinished sentences hold the turn open.** h23 "…at the end of the" got 3.2–3.4 s of lag. h24 and h26 were
  held until the next line started, so the two lines merged.
- **Splits:** h02 ("…no wait," | "forty-two") in r1 quiet and r2 music, h19 in r2 music, h18 every quiet run.
  The rolling group repaired the music r2 splits (h02, h19), but not h02 in quiet r1, where the 1 s rule also pulled
  h03 into the group and the reading kept bar 40.
- **Merges (quiet only):**
  - h24 + h25, r1: "Sopranos at bar sixty you yes you bar eighty eight too loud" became "Too loud 60–88" for the
    Sopranos, which is dangerous.
  - h26 + h27, both runs: answered none, which is correct for both lines.
  - h17 + h18, both runs: "Violas" was dropped from the turn text, so h18 lost its audience and both readings
    shipped an ensemble dim. or cresc. at 30.
- **Music file:** the endpointer did better, with 27/27 turns in r1, presumably because the music bed keeps it
  from deciding on room-tone silences.
- **Words lost at the edges:** about the same as the batch clip transcripts (P2 3–5 at the start and 0–4 at the
  end over 27 lines, against P1 3–6 and 1–2). The one that mattered is "Violas" at h18.
- **The rolling group helped on music** (51/54 against 48/54) and not on quiet (47/54 both). In music r2 it grouped
  h23's unfinished turn with h24 and wrote a phantom Viola mark at 60.
- **End of speech to proposal:** 2.9–3.0 s median for P2, against 3.7 s for P1 (ASR 1.0–1.5 s plus Spark) and
  2.2 s for P3a.
